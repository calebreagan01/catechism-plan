This plan is meant for a person in the first years of following Jesus. It walks through the whole Bible in 900 days, stops now and then to teach the beliefs of the Christian faith, and tries to show you how to read Scripture for yourself. I’ve called it Catechism. A catechism is a tool for teaching new believers what Christians believe, and that is the job I hope this plan does.

## Where I Am Coming From

I believe the Bible is the word of God, true and trustworthy from beginning to end. I believe in one God in three persons, in the full deity and full humanity of Jesus, in his death in the place of sinners and his bodily resurrection, and in salvation by grace alone through faith alone in Christ alone. My reading of Scripture leans toward the sovereignty of God in salvation and the sufficiency of Christ, and I do not want to hide that.

The framework behind the plan is what some call progressive covenantalism. Big terms like that can sometimes overcomplicate things, but I think the idea is fairly simple. The Bible tells one story, and God tells it through a series of covenants: with creation and Adam, with Noah, with Abraham, with Israel at Sinai, with David, and finally the new covenant in Christ. Each covenant builds on the one before it and points ahead to the next. Jesus fulfills them all.

That idea explains the shape of the plan. Year 1, Promise and Shadow, moves through the covenants one at a time. Year 2, Fulfillment, follows Jesus, the church, and the new creation. Many of the units carry the names of covenants. The Connection section of a daily guide asks how a passage leads to Christ. The middle question on each Sabbath day is always about him. These are choices that come straight from the framework.

Not every Christian reads the Bible this way. Some hold that God has one covenant of grace unfolding through history, with Israel and the church closely joined. Others distinguish sharply between God’s purposes for Israel and for the church. Those traditions have produced faithful, Bible-loving people, and they will sometimes read a passage differently than I do. Where it matters I have tried to say so. Where I have not, you may need to notice it yourself.

I have also tried to avoid the private vocabulary of any one camp. The goal is accessible depth: enough substance to feed a growing believer, without requiring you to join a side before you can read a page. My hope is that through this plan, you will feel equipped to open your Bible and know what to do next. God’s Word is living, and I want you to love it, learn it, and live by it.

## Decisions You Should Know About

A plan like this makes hundreds of choices on the reader’s behalf. Most are invisible. These are the ones I think you should see.

**The story is organized by covenant.** The units follow the covenants, not the order of the books in your Bible. That is a way of reading the Bible, not the only way.

**The reading order is a decision.** The plan is not in canonical order. Job sits between Abraham and the Exodus, following the common view that its setting is patriarchal, though the book does not date itself and scholars differ. The Psalms are woven through the whole plan. The Wisdom books come after David and Solomon. The Gospels are read in the order Matthew, Luke, John, and Mark. Hebrews comes before the General Epistles and Revelation. Other orders are reasonable, and where the date or authorship of a book is debated, the Books of the Bible section of Appendix II says so.

**Christ is the center of every part.** Jesus taught that the Law, the Prophets, and the Psalms all speak of him (Luke 24:44). I have read the Old Testament that way throughout. Some readers will feel that I find Christ in places where the original author may not have seen him clearly. I have tried to lean on how the New Testament itself reads the Old, and to be modest where it does not.

**The doctrinal pauses reflect my judgment.** The 44 doctrinal days cover what I think a new believer most needs, plus the teachings the story itself raises. Where Christians have long agreed, I state it plainly. Where faithful Christians differ, I try to describe the positions fairly and say that they differ. I will not always have kept my own view out of it.

**The weekly rhythm is built into the calendar.** Six days of reading are followed by a day of rest, and the unit boundaries bend around that rhythm. The rest day falls on Saturday only because the plan begins on a Sunday. How Christians should keep the Sabbath or the Lord’s Day is a question on which they differ, and the plan does not settle it.

**Every guide is a selection.** Each daily guide is short, and it leaves out far more than it includes. Choosing what to notice in a passage is already interpretation. Another teacher would choose differently, and the passage is always richer than the guide.

**The translation is the English Standard Version.** The guides refer to the ESV. Any faithful translation will serve you, and reading in more than one is a good habit.
