This plan walks you through the whole Bible in 900 days, about two and a half years, and stops along the way to teach you the main beliefs of the Christian faith and how to study Scripture for yourself. You do not need any background. You need a Bible, a quiet fifteen to twenty-five minutes, and a willingness to keep coming back.

## The Shape of the Plan

The plan has two halves. Year 1, Promise and Shadow, covers Units 1 to 14 and follows the story from creation through the Old Testament and the promises God makes. Year 2, Fulfillment, covers Units 15 to 25 and follows Jesus, the church, and the hope of the new creation. Each unit has its own page with a contents list, and every day has its own page.

Weeks run from Sunday to Saturday. Six days of reading are followed by a Sabbath rest on Saturday, so every seventh day is lighter. The Weekly Checklist lists all 129 weeks so you can see where you are, and it keeps track of the days you mark as finished.

## A Normal Day

Each reading day follows the same pattern, so you always know what to expect.

- Reading: the passage for the day. Read it slowly, and aloud if you can.
- Setting the Scene: who wrote this, who it was written to, and what is going on.
- Observation: questions that help you notice what the text actually says.
- Interpretation: what the passage means in its own setting.
- Connection: how the passage points to Jesus and fits the whole Bible.
- Application: what to believe, change, or do in response.
- Prayer: two or three prompts to help you respond to God in your own words. The week’s memory verse closes each day as a reminder.
Read the passage first, then the guide. If you have more time, read the passage twice before you open the commentary. The guide is there to help you see the text, not to replace it.

## Special Days

Sabbath days come every Saturday. You do not read a new passage. You meditate on the memory verse, look back over the week, and answer three reflection questions, the middle one always about Christ. Treat the day as rest, not catch-up.

Doctrinal pauses interrupt the story to study one teaching of the faith, such as the Trinity or justification. They carry an extra section called the Doctrinal Focus. The Doctrines page lists all 44.

Unit reviews close each unit. They recap what you learned, bring back the unit anchor verse, and ask questions that help it stick. Three reviews fall on a Saturday and combine the review with the Sabbath.

## Memory Verses

Each unit has one anchor verse. You do not have to memorize it perfectly. Read it each Saturday, say it aloud a few times, and let it settle. All 25 are listed on the Memory Verses page, together with each week’s Sabbath verse.

## If You Fall Behind

You will. Everyone does. Do not try to read three days at once. Pick up at the next day and keep going. The checklist is a record, not a grade. If you miss a Sabbath, skip it and move on. If you miss a long stretch, start again at the beginning of the current unit and read at a gentler pace. A plan you finish slowly is better than one you abandon.

## Studying the Bible on Your Own

The daily guides use a simple three-part method you can use anywhere in the Bible.

- Observe: what does the text say? Note who, what, where, when, and the words that repeat.
- Interpret: what did it mean to its first readers? Read the verses around it before you decide.
- Apply: what does it call me to believe, love, or do?
Add one more question every time: where is Christ in this passage? Jesus taught that the whole Bible speaks of him (Luke 24:27). The question will not always have a quick answer, but asking it will change how you read.

## Reading With Others

This plan works well in a small group, a family, or with one friend. Agree on a day each week, usually the Sabbath, to talk about the week. Share one thing you noticed, one thing you did not understand, and one way you will respond. Write your questions down. Bring the hard ones to a pastor or a mature Christian. Confusion is a normal part of learning.

## Bible Translation

The guides quote and refer to the English Standard Version (ESV), but any faithful translation will work. They give references and brief quotations, but they do not print the full passage, so keep your Bible open.
